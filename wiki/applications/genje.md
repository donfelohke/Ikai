# GENJE and embodied computing

**Status: RESEARCH / CREATIVE PRACTICE.** GENJE is not merely an IKAI feature. It is an independent creative and cultural domain that may use reflective and computational environments.

## The embodied proposition

A person is not simply an object to be sensed. Movement is intentional, contextual and expressive. GENJE investigates relationships between music, movement, choreography, live coding and algorithmic systems.

A possible interaction loop is **Movement → Sensing → Representation → Transformation → Sound / Visual output → Human response**. The performer perceives the output and chooses how to respond. The loop is collaborative and contingent, not an automatic judgement of the performer.

## Choreohacking

Choreohacking treats movement structures and choreographic rules as inspectable and modifiable. Sequences, constraints, rhythms and transitions can be recombined, improvised and executed. This is a creative research proposition, not a claim that movement can be fully represented by a finite feature vector.

## Laban-related research

Laban Movement Analysis offers established vocabularies for examining Body, Effort, Shape and Space. A computational representation inspired by selected descriptors may be useful, but the proposed LabanLite intermediate representation remains experimental until its semantics, limits and usefulness are demonstrated.

Movement descriptors must not be used to infer identity, personality, emotion, health or worth.

## Possible technical connections

SuperCollider, MIDI, OSC, TidalCycles, Hydra, Guile/Scheme, OpenCV and selected motion-sensing libraries are candidates for investigation. Selection must consider licence, provenance, latency, local operation, maintainability and the actual creative requirement.

## Cultural authorship

African movement and music must not be stripped of context and treated as anonymous training material. Research should preserve provenance, authorship, community context, consent and the distinction between a cultural practice and a computational representation of selected features.

## Relationship to IKAI

IKAI may provide reflective surfaces, local computation and participant-directed interaction. GENJE provides creative grammars, movement practice and performance questions. Neither domain should absorb the other's identity or governance responsibilities.
