# Source Evaluation and Citation Protocol

## Source classes

- **Primary evidence:** datasets, original experiments, archival records, standards text, source code, direct measurements, interviews, or original documentation.
- **Official technical documentation:** manuals, specifications, release notes, issue trackers, and project-maintainer documentation.
- **Peer-reviewed research:** original studies, systematic reviews, meta-analyses, and scholarly critique.
- **Scholarly and institutional synthesis:** books, surveys, institutional reports, and critical histories.
- **Journalism and public commentary:** useful for chronology, public claims, and leads; verify technical or historical assertions where possible.
- **Community knowledge:** practitioner reports, forums, oral histories, and lived experience; preserve context and avoid treating a single account as universal.
- **AI-generated material:** a discovery or drafting aid only; not evidence unless its underlying sources are independently checked.

## Evaluate each source

Record, where applicable:

1. Author or responsible institution.
2. Title and stable URL, DOI, archive identifier, or repository commit.
3. Publication date, version, and access date where relevant.
4. Source type and proximity to the original evidence.
5. Method, sample, assumptions, and scope.
6. Funding, conflicts of interest, or relevant institutional context.
7. Limitations, corrections, retractions, and credible counterevidence.
8. Licence, privacy constraints, and permission to redistribute.

## Citation practice

- Cite material factual claims close to the claim.
- Prefer a stable source identifier over a search-result snippet.
- For software behaviour, cite versioned documentation, source code, tests, or a reproducible issue.
- For numerical results, preserve units, denominator, dates, and calculation method.
- For historical claims, seek corroboration and distinguish archival evidence from later interpretation.
- For oral or culturally held knowledge, record consent and attribution preferences; do not publish restricted knowledge simply because it was encountered.
- If a source cannot be accessed or checked, say so.

## Bibliography entry template

```text
Author / institution:
Title:
Source type:
Publication date / version:
Stable URL / DOI / commit:
Access date (if relevant):
Claim(s) supported:
Method / scope:
Limitations / counterevidence:
Licence / permissions:
Verification status:
```

## Research integrity

Do not fabricate citations, page numbers, quotations, datasets, experiments, or test results. Quote only what has been checked against the source. Preserve relevant disagreements and update entries when a source is corrected or withdrawn.
