# Before Africa — Existence, Personhood and Knowledge

**IKAI research note · Updated 11 November 2026 · Status: RESEARCH**

## Research question

How have particular African intellectual traditions described existence, personhood, relation, moral responsibility, nature, ancestry, time, knowledge and the good life—and how can these traditions be studied without reducing them to one continental philosophy?

The parent research programme is the FELOH Sovereign Corpus *Before Africa* dossier.

## Comparative discipline

“African philosophy” and “Africana philosophy” are broad scholarly fields, not names for one uniform doctrine. Concepts must be attached to language, community, period, genre and source. Ancient Egyptian texts, southern African ubuntu/botho/hunhu traditions, Islamic scholarship in African societies, Ethiopian intellectual traditions and oral philosophical practice have distinct histories and cannot be treated as interchangeable.

Examples for carefully scoped study:
- Ancient Egyptian discussions of *ma’at*, order, justice and the cosmos, read through relevant texts and specialist scholarship.
- Ubuntu/botho/hunhu debates concerning personhood, dignity, relationship and moral life in particular southern African languages and intellectual contexts.
- Philosophical reasoning in oral traditions, proverbs, narrative, law, debate and social practice.
- Islamic, Christian and other intellectual traditions as historically situated African formations, including exchange and contestation.

“Livity” is a useful contemporary comparative term in this project but is strongly associated with Rastafari history and should not be projected onto all African societies or ancient texts.

The corpus maintains a strict distinction: deep African ancestry and cultural continuity are real; the modern racial category “Black” is a historical formation that must not be projected unchanged into prehistory.

## IKAI connection: mirror, not judge

IKAI's design principle—support reflection without scoring, diagnosing, identifying or covertly profiling a person—can be placed in dialogue with traditions that examine personhood, ethical responsibility and relational life. This is a contemporary design question, not proof that historical traditions anticipated digital interfaces.

Research should ask: who defines the categories? Who can inspect the record? What is the context? Who controls interpretation? What knowledge should not be captured? How can a system preserve agency and allow refusal?

No cultural tradition should be converted into a behavioural metric, personality score or biometric profile. Respect communal custodianship and restrictions on sacred or private knowledge.

Tempo agency, interpretive openness, protected idleness and ethical non-extraction (IKAI research dimensions) remain design constraints that protect human judgement rather than replace it.

## Method

Use primary texts in original language where possible, critical translations, oral sources, historical context and contemporary scholarship. Record translation choices and disagreements. Avoid presenting a proverb or isolated quotation as proof of an entire worldview.

Link population-genetic and archaeological findings (Takarkori, southern African genomes, regional Holocene sequences) only where they illuminate the historical conditions of knowledge production; do not reduce intellectual traditions to genetics.

For any dialogue between historical traditions and IKAI:
- Attach concepts to a named language, community, period, text or documented oral context.
- Preserve original-language terms and identify translation choices where possible.
- Do not infer a historical worldview from an isolated proverb or quotation.
- Do not reduce a tradition, personhood concept or ancestry model to a behavioural score, personality metric, biometric profile or computational ontology.
- Treat genomic evidence as population history, not as a measure of intelligence, personhood, moral worth or cultural authenticity.
- Preserve refusal, privacy, custodianship and the right not to have knowledge captured.
