# Reflective computing

**Status: RESEARCH / CONCEPT.** This article consolidates the central proposition. It is not a claim of a validated implementation.

## From reflection to computation

A conventional mirror returns light and presents a reflected view. A display placed behind partially reflective glass adds controlled visual output. A camera introduces an input channel. Computation can connect selected inputs and outputs, producing an interactive environment.

**Light IN → Reflection → Light OUT** expands to **Light IN → Sense / Compute → Light OUT**.

The important change is not that a mirror becomes a computer in the abstract. It is that a physical reflective environment can become an input/output surface where human-directed interaction, sensing, computation and visual feedback are deliberately coordinated.

## Four distinct layers

1. **Optical layer** — mirror substrate, coating, transmission, reflection, illumination and viewing geometry.
2. **I/O layer** — camera, touch, audio and other explicitly selected input/output devices.
3. **Computational layer** — capture, processing, state, rendering, persistence and device coordination.
4. **Human and governance layer** — intent, consent, visibility, authority, interpretation and control.

A successful display demonstration does not validate camera quality, participant benefit or privacy.

## Bidirectional interaction

A reflective computing environment may receive input, process it and return output. The person can perceive that output and decide whether to respond. This creates a feedback loop: **Observe → Interpret → Present → Human decides → Act → Observe**.

The person remains an active participant, not merely a sensor target. The system must not infer identity, health, emotion, personality, worth or capability from reflected appearance or movement.

## Mirror, Glass and Space

- **Mirror:** the reflective surface and its optical properties.
- **Glass:** transparent or partially reflective interfaces and wearable display possibilities.
- **Space:** spatial registration, multiple surfaces and immersive environments.

These are research tracks, not implemented product modes.

## System relationship

NAKEBUSEN provides an initial real-world beauty-service context. WARENA provides a potential technical capability and infrastructure base. IKAI investigates the reflective application and participant experience. GENJE independently develops embodied creative practice that may inform or use reflective interaction systems.
