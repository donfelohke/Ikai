# IKAI — Reflective Computing Copy Guide

**Domain:** MIRROR / REFLECTION.  
**Maturity:** RESEARCH / WORK IN PROGRESS — not a released product.

## Position

**IKAI — A Mirror, Not a Judge.**

IKAI investigates reflective computing: the integration of reflective optics, sensing, displays, local computation and human-directed interaction in physical environments. The mirror is an experimental embodiment, not the limit of the research.

## Short and long descriptions

**Short:** IKAI researches reflective computing and embodied interaction with human authority, consent, context and local control at the centre.

**Long:** IKAI studies how optical input, sensing, computation, display output and human feedback can form a coherent, measurable and participant-governed environment. Research distinguishes acquisition, sensing, computation, rendering, timing, calibration, interpretation and validation. Observations are evidence with context—not automatic truth.

## Public copy

**A MIRROR, NOT A JUDGE.**  
Reflective computing research for human-directed interaction, local control and embodied understanding.

Explore the research questions, architecture and validation plans. IKAI remains research / work in progress; the M1 mirror is an engineering candidate, not a released or fully integrated product.

## Principles

- Human remains author.
- Mirror is not judge.
- Observation is not truth.
- Consent governs capture and sensing.
- Minimise data; prefer local processing.
- Make retention, export and deletion visible and testable.
- Distinguish hypothesis, design, prototype, validation and release.

## Advertising and design restrictions

Do not market IKAI as a smart mirror, beauty mirror, AI mirror, diagnostic device or autonomous personal-assessment system. Do not imply face identification, biometric identity matching, person scoring, diagnosis, covert recording, hidden profiling or cloud profiling.

Visuals should show reflective environments, optics, interfaces and human interaction with context. Avoid face-scoring overlays, biometric targeting, neon AI HUDs and visuals implying capabilities that have not been implemented.

## Technical copy standards

Describe the full pipeline and its boundaries: acquisition, sensing, computation, rendering, timing, calibration, feedback, storage and participant control. For distributed spatial work, disclose uncertainty, clock drift, tracking loss, network partition and map disagreement rather than hiding them behind a single authoritative scene.

A component list, diagram or proposed architecture is not an integrated prototype. State the exact validation status and evidence.
