# Black Hinterland — Existence, Knowledge Governance and Reflective Futures

**IKAI / MIRROR research note · 22 November 2026 · Status: RESEARCH**  
**Canonical framework:** [FELOH Corpus — BLACK HINTERLAND: BEFORE AFRICA](https://github.com/donfelohke/feloh-corpus/blob/main/docs/BLACK-HINTERLAND-BEFORE-AFRICA-MASTER-DOSSIER-2026-11-22.md)

## Scope

IKAI's bounded contribution concerns existence, personhood, relation, memory, reflection, knowledge governance and participant agency. It asks how particular African intellectual traditions and contemporary communities describe the human person, ethical responsibility, ancestry, place, time, nature and the good life—and how records and interfaces can preserve agency rather than impose categories.

There is no single timeless African philosophy. Concepts must be attached to a language, community, period, text or documented oral context. Ancient Egyptian materials, southern African ubuntu/botho/hunhu debates, Islamic and Christian intellectual traditions in African societies, Ethiopian scholarship, and oral philosophical practice have distinct histories.

## Research questions

- Who names a person, community, place or tradition, and with what authority?
- Who controls the recording, translation, storage, interpretation and circulation of knowledge?
- How should disagreement, context, uncertainty, refusal and restricted knowledge be represented?
- What can an archive preserve, and what should remain unrecorded or community-controlled?
- How can reflective systems support human interpretation without scoring, diagnosing, identifying or profiling people?

## IKAI boundary

IKAI remains a mirror, not a judge. Do not convert cultural knowledge, ancestry, language, movement or philosophical traditions into biometric identity, behavioural scoring, personality metrics or automated judgements. No hidden recording, default cloud profile or undisclosed transfer. Preserve consent, data minimisation, local processing, inspection, correction, export and refusal.

A design correspondence between a historical ethical concept and IKAI is a contemporary interpretation—not evidence that a historical community anticipated digital computing. Genomic ancestry cannot measure intelligence, personhood, moral worth or cultural authenticity.

## Sources and method

Use primary texts in original languages where possible, critical translations, African scholarship, oral-history methods and community-approved testimony. Record translation choices, custodianship, disagreement, consent and access restrictions.
