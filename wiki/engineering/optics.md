# Two-way mirror and optical engineering

**Status: RESEARCH. Preferred initial optical direction: two-way mirror assembly.**

## Optical principle

A partially reflective and partially transmissive element can support a reflected view while allowing light from a display behind it to pass toward the viewer. The observed result depends on coating, substrate, display luminance, ambient illumination, viewing angle, enclosure geometry and relative scene brightness.

“Two-way mirror” is a common product description, not a complete engineering specification. Procurement must be based on measurable reflectance, transmittance, colour neutrality, flatness, thickness, safety and mounting characteristics.

## Main trade-offs

| Variable | Engineering consequence |
|---|---|
| Reflectance | Influences mirror appearance and reflected-scene visibility |
| Transmittance | Influences how much display light reaches the viewer |
| Display luminance | Must be adequate after optical losses and ambient-light competition |
| Ambient illumination | Can wash out displayed content or alter perceived reflection |
| Viewing angle | Can change perceived brightness, colour and reflection |
| Coating and substrate | Affect colour cast, ghosting, distortion and durability |
| Enclosure | Affects alignment, heat, serviceability and safety |

No universal reflectance/transmittance ratio is asserted. The correct specification depends on the installation and must be measured using samples.

## M1 experimental sequence

1. Obtain documented samples with known optical specifications.
2. Record display luminance and test ambient-light conditions.
3. Measure reflected and transmitted light with a repeatable method.
4. Assess colour shift and image distortion.
5. Test viewing-angle variation.
6. Evaluate mounting, edge treatment, ventilation and cleaning.
7. Preserve photographs and measurement conditions with each result.

## Camera placement is separate

A camera mounted behind the optical element may encounter transmission loss, reflections, ghosting, colour shift, focus changes and internal reflections. An external camera is a useful baseline for isolating software and interaction questions. Behind-glass capture should be tested separately.

Camera-to-reflection registration requires geometry calibration. The camera viewpoint is not automatically the same as the viewer's reflected viewpoint.

## Safety and maintainability

Account for glass handling, edge protection, mechanical retention, heat, electrical isolation, repair access and failure behaviour. A prototype should not be installed in a customer-facing environment until appropriate safety and operational reviews are complete.
