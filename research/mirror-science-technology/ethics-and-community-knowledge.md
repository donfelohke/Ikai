# Research Ethics, Community Knowledge, and Publication Safeguards

**Status:** PROPOSED POLICY BASELINE — requires domain-specific review before use in a real study.

## Purpose

MIRROR research may concern people, movement, cultural practice, language, historical archives, community-held knowledge, or technical systems that affect people. These contexts require more than citation and technical reproducibility.

## Questions before collecting or publishing

1. What is the purpose, and is collection necessary?
2. Who may be affected by collection, interpretation, publication, or reuse?
3. What consent, legal basis, community permission, institutional approval, or other safeguards are required?
4. Who controls access, and who benefits?
5. What information is sensitive, identifying, restricted, sacred, confidential, or unsafe to disclose?
6. Can a participant or community withdraw, correct, contextualise, or restrict material where appropriate?
7. How long will records be retained, who can access them, and how will they be protected or deleted?
8. What are the risks of re-identification, decontextualisation, appropriation, or harmful secondary use?
9. Are translation, transcription, and editorial choices recorded and reviewable?
10. Does publication imply authority or consensus that the source material does not support?

## Human-participant safeguards

- Define purpose and data minimisation before collection.
- Explain what will be recorded, how it will be used, who may access it, and what cannot be guaranteed.
- Do not treat a public appearance, public social-media post, or technically accessible record as automatic permission for any secondary use.
- Do not infer sensitive traits, identity, diagnosis, intention, or psychological state from movement or behavioural data without a defensible, ethically reviewed basis.
- Prefer local processing and short retention where appropriate, but do not assume local storage alone makes a study safe.
- Use synthetic data for early system tests when it can answer the technical question.
- Seek qualified ethics review when required by law, institution, funder, field standards, or risk profile.

## Community-held and Indigenous knowledge

- Ask whose knowledge is being recorded and who is authorised to grant permission.
- Respect community protocols, attribution preferences, language, context, and restrictions on circulation.
- Distinguish an individual informant's account from a community-wide position.
- Preserve disagreement and variation; do not force one account to stand for an entire people or region.
- Do not equate lack of written documentation with lack of knowledge.
- Do not publish restricted or sensitive material merely because it can be cited or digitised.
- Clarify rights to access, storage, translation, derivative works, commercial use, and future reuse before collecting material.
- Where appropriate, return useful records to contributing people or communities in accessible formats.

## AI and reflective-compute boundary

MIRROR / IKAI research must not quietly convert reflection into surveillance or authority. Proposed systems should explicitly address face identification and biometric matching; person scoring, ranking, diagnosis, or covert profiling; sensitive-trait inference; third-party processing, telemetry, network calls, and retention; human review, contestability, correction, export and deletion; and data-flow transparency.

A policy statement alone does not establish that software respects these boundaries. Implemented controls require tests and evidence.

## Publication decision checklist

- [ ] Source provenance and permission are documented.
- [ ] Licence and third-party rights are checked.
- [ ] Identifying and sensitive data are minimised.
- [ ] Community or participant restrictions are respected.
- [ ] Re-identification and harmful secondary-use risks are assessed.
- [ ] Access conditions and retention are stated.
- [ ] Translation and interpretation limits are documented.
- [ ] No unsupported claim of representativeness or consensus is made.

## Limits

This document is a research governance starting point, not legal advice, institutional ethics approval, or a substitute for community-specific protocols. Applicable Kenyan law, institutional requirements, and relevant community permissions must be checked for each real project.
