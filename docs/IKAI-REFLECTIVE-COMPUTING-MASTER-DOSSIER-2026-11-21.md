# IKAI — Reflective Computing Master Research Dossier

**Date:** 21 November 2026  
**Status:** RESEARCH / CONSOLIDATION — not a product specification or validation report.

## 1. Executive thesis

IKAI investigates reflective computing: integration of reflective optics, sensing, displays, local computation and human-directed interaction in physical environments.

The foundational progression is conventional mirror: Light IN → reflection → Light OUT; expanded to Light IN → optional sensing / computation → Light OUT, with a human-directed feedback loop.

A mirror is the first physical research embodiment, not the entire category. The longer direction includes reflective environments, camera-screen interaction, multimodal I/O and possible spatial/Mirror XR applications.

**Core principle: a mirror, not a judge.** The system may help a person inspect, reflect and create; it must not identify, rank, diagnose, score or covertly profile people.

## 3. Research architecture

### Optical layer

Investigate reflective/transmissive elements, substrate, coating, display placement, luminance, contrast, ambient light, viewing angle, colour neutrality, distortion, enclosure and safety. Two-way mirror glass is the preferred initial M1 research direction, subject to measurement and procurement validation.

### I/O layer

Investigate cameras, touch, audio and selected sensors as distinct, optional channels. Camera availability is not consent. Camera-to-reflection geometry must be calibrated; the camera viewpoint is not automatically equivalent to the reflected viewpoint.

### Real-time compute layer

Investigate capture, buffering, processing, synchronisation, rendering, device management, local persistence and recovery. Linux candidates include V4L2, Media Controller, GStreamer, DRM/KMS, ALSA and appropriate native device interfaces. These are candidates, not a final selected stack.

### Human and governance layer

Make intent, consent, recording state, permissions, retention, export, deletion and authority visible and testable. The participant decides what to capture, retain, share and act upon.

## 4. Engineering development stages

- **M1 — Optical proof:** mirror sample, display and repeatable optical tests.
- **M2 — Bidirectional ReflectiveIO:** camera, touch and display integration experiments.
- **M3 — Contextual reflective environment:** local processing, state and selected embodied interactions.
- **M4 — Mirror XR / distributed reflective environments:** spatial registration, multiple surfaces and immersive experiments.

These are proposed stages, not completed milestones. Prove optical behaviour before committing to complex sensing or XR.

## 5. Application research

### NAKEBUSEN Beauty Tech

Candidate workflows include consultation references, client-directed visual documentation, comparison under controlled lighting, care information and user-controlled service continuity. Do not infer health or diagnose hair/scalp conditions from images. Commercial value must be established through a baseline workflow, practitioner/client feedback, measured benefit and willingness to pay.

### GENJE embodied computing

Investigate movement, music, choreography, live coding and algorithmic expression. A possible loop is movement → sensing → representation → transformation → sound/visual output → human response. Laban-related descriptors and the proposed LabanLite IR remain research ideas; they are not validated standards and must not be used to infer identity, emotion, health or personality.

## 6. Research and validation requirements

A demonstration is not validation. Record research question, hypothesis, hardware/software, source commit, data, consent basis, processing location, procedure, success/failure criteria, observations, limitations, alternative explanations, privacy review, reproduction steps and deletion confirmation.

Critical measurements include optical reflectance/transmittance, display readability, colour shift, viewing angle, camera quality, camera/display registration, frame loss, latency distributions, synchronisation, thermal behaviour, sustained reliability and recovery.

## 7. Open research questions

- What optical specification best balances reflection and display transmission?
- Can camera capture through the selected glass meet intended requirements?
- What camera position and calibration method are appropriate?
- Which touch approach survives the optical/mechanical assembly?
- What is the end-to-end latency under sustained load?
- Which existing interfaces cover requirements without a new ABI?
- What is the minimum data required for each application?
- Does the beauty workflow produce measurable benefit?
- Which movement representations preserve context and performer agency?
- What practical advantage does Mirror XR offer over conventional displays or XR devices?

## 8. Non-goals and safeguards

No face identification, biometric matching, person scoring, ranking, diagnosis, covert recording, hidden profiling or undisclosed external transfer. No default cloud profile. No claim of clinical validity, privacy validation, production readiness, product-market fit or commercial availability without appropriate evidence.

## 10. Commercial and maturity boundary

IKAI remains research / work in progress. No IKAI TAM, revenue forecast, validated customer benefit or released product is asserted. All commercial enquiries and transactions remain with NAKEBUSEN. Every public capability claim must be traceable to implementation and test evidence.
